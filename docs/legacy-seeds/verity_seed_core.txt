3C. Route, Screen, and ViewModel Ownership (Clarification — Locked)

Purpose:
To remove ambiguity around Compose Route patterns and ViewModel ownership.

Definitions:

• Route:
  – A composable boundary responsible for:
    • Obtaining ViewModels from the composition root
    • Collecting StateFlow / Flow
    • Passing state and callbacks downward
  – Routes MAY reference ViewModels.
  – Routes MUST NOT create business logic or draft mutations.

• Screen:
  – A pure UI renderer.
  – Receives state and callbacks only.
  – MUST NOT obtain or create ViewModels.
  – MUST NOT collect Flows directly from repositories or stores.

• ViewModel:
  – Owned by the application or navigation root (e.g. MainActivity / NavHost).
  – Injected into Routes, never into Screens.
  – Features MUST NOT instantiate their own ViewModels implicitly.

Structural Rules (Non‑Negotiable):

• MainActivity / NavHost is the authoritative lifecycle owner of ViewModels.
• Features may define ViewModel types but do not control their instantiation.
• Preview helpers and fake data are UI-only and MUST NOT leak into app or platform layers.
• Routes exist to bridge lifecycle‑aware state into pure screens.

Violation of these rules is an architectural defect.

4A. Immutability Laws (Frozen)

• The events table is append-only.
• No event may be updated, deleted, or rewritten.
• All corrections are represented by new compensating events.
• No migration may alter the meaning of historical events.
• Projections and drafts are disposable; events are not.
• Replay is the only valid mechanism to derive state from history.
• Immutability requirements apply strictly to financial and document
  domain truth; supporting identity data may evolve via CRUD without
  violating historical correctness guarantees.

Any attempt to bypass immutability is a correctness violation.

4B. Aggregate Boundaries (Frozen)

Verity uses explicit aggregate boundaries to enforce correctness and prevent cross-domain mutation.
An aggregate is the smallest unit that enforces its own invariants and emits its own domain events.

Authoritative aggregate list (Phase 5):

• Customer
  - Identity: customerId
  - Responsibility: customer identity and non-financial metadata
  - Persistence Model: CRUD-managed (not event-sourced)

• Invoice
  - Identity: invoiceId
  - Responsibility: financial debit (service or sale)
  - Emits: InvoiceFinalized, InvoiceCancelled

• Challan
  - Identity: challanId
  - Responsibility: goods custody / logistical truth
  - Emits: ChallanIssued, ChallanCancelled

• Payment
  - Identity: paymentId
  - Responsibility: financial credit
  - Emits: PaymentRecorded, PaymentReversed

• DocumentRelationship
  - Identity: relationshipId
  - Responsibility: explicit, auditable linkage between domain documents
  - Emits: DocumentsLinked (and compensating unlink events if introduced later)

Rules:
• Aggregates validate commands and decide truth.
• Aggregates must never read or mutate other aggregates.
• Cross-aggregate coordination is expressed only via events and references.
• Ledger, balances, statuses, jobs, drafts, and summaries are NOT aggregates; they are projections or workflow state.
• Aggregates emit events only after successful invariant validation; drafts and UI state are not events.

Changing aggregate boundaries requires an explicit constitutional amendment.

5D. Focus & IME Management Law (LOCKED)

Purpose
To ensure fast, predictable, and professional data entry for power users,
especially in high‑frequency workflows such as invoice creation.

Law — Explicit Focus Ownership

• Focus management MUST be explicit and intentional.
• Focus logic MUST live at the screen / caller level.
• Layout containers, surfaces, or edit shells MUST NOT own focus logic.

Rules

• Expandable edit blocks:
  – MUST auto‑focus the first logical input when expanded.
  – MUST clear focus and dismiss IME when collapsed.

• Field‑to‑field navigation:
  – MUST be linear and deterministic.
  – MUST be implemented via IME actions (Next / Done).
  – Edit containers MUST NOT infer or impose field order.

• IME semantics:
  – Each input MUST declare its intent via ImeAction.
  – “Next” advances focus.
  – “Done” terminates the edit session or dismisses IME.

• Scope of responsibility:
  – Screens define focus order and IME behavior.
  – Molecules (e.g. VerityEditBlock) provide structure only.
  – Primitives (e.g. VerityTextField) remain stateless and controlled.

Forbidden Patterns

• Internal focus state inside edit blocks or containers.
• Implicit or heuristic focus movement.
• Relying on default IME behavior for multi‑field editors.
• Using edit‑mode flags to compensate for missing focus ownership.

Rationale

Fast‑entry UX is a first‑class requirement in Verity.
Predictable focus and keyboard behavior are mandatory for correctness,
speed, and long‑session ergonomics.

Purpose
To prevent UI instability, cursor snap‑back, deletion failures, and ambiguous state ownership in Compose forms.

Law — Single Ownership of TextField Value

• Every editable TextField **MUST have exactly one owner of its `value`.**
• The owner is always **UI‑local input/query state**.
• Draft, domain, or projection state **MUST NEVER drive a TextField’s `value`.**

Violation is a correctness defect.

Autocomplete & Selection Rules

• TextField `value` MUST always be bound to query/input state.
• Selecting a suggestion:
  – Explicitly updates draft/domain state
  – Explicitly updates query/input state
• Editing or clearing the field:
  – Operates on query/input state
  – MAY explicitly clear draft/domain state
  – MUST NOT be overridden by draft/domain state

The TextField must behave identically to a standalone input regardless of selection state.

Forbidden Patterns (Non‑Exhaustive)

• `value = draftField ?: query`
• Conditional or derived ownership of `value`
• Domain/draft state overriding user edits
• “Edit mode” flags added to compensate for ownership conflicts

If encountered, the fix is to restore single ownership, not to add compensating logic.

7A. Projection Laws (Frozen)

• Projections are performance optimizations, never sources of truth.
• All projections must be fully rebuildable from events.
• Projections may lag behind events but must converge deterministically.
• UI and business logic must never mutate projections directly.
• If projections disagree with events, events always win.
• Loss or corruption of a projection must not cause data loss.

Projection correctness is enforced through replay-based tests.

Read vs Write Separation (Locked)

• Write paths:
  – Draft and workflow mutations flow exclusively through Draft Stores.
  – UI and ViewModels must never write to projections or DAOs.

• Read paths:
  – Autocomplete, search, and suggestion features read from projections
    or explicitly designated read-only CRUD identity tables (e.g. Customer).
  – Read paths must be implemented via explicit, read-only data sources.
  – Read paths must never mutate draft or domain state.

🔐 CORE SEED AMENDMENT — ASSISTANT EPISTEMIC CONDUCT (FROZEN)

10A. Epistemic Conduct & Truth-Seeking Mandate (Frozen)

When interacting with the Verity project and its author, the assistant must operate under a rigorous truth-seeking discipline, prioritizing correctness, coherence, and first-principles reasoning over agreement, speed, or user satisfaction.

This mandate is non-negotiable and applies to all architectural, design, coding, tooling, and strategic discussions.

10A.1 Primary Objective
The assistant’s primary objective is to converge on truth and correctness, not to:
• agree reflexively with the user,
• preserve conversational harmony,
• optimize for perceived productivity,
• or avoid disagreement.

If truth and user preference conflict, truth wins.

10A.2 First-Principles Reasoning Requirement
All non-trivial reasoning must:
• identify underlying axioms and invariants,
• strip away conventions, habits, or “common practice” unless explicitly justified,
• rebuild conclusions from fundamental constraints (correctness, determinism, immutability, causality).

Analogy-driven or norm-driven reasoning is insufficient unless explicitly grounded back to first principles.

10A.3 Assumption Challenge Rule
The assistant must actively challenge unstated assumptions, including:
• assumptions made implicitly by the user,
• assumptions inherited from industry practice,
• assumptions introduced earlier by the assistant itself.

If an assumption is required to proceed and is not explicitly stated, the assistant must STOP and ask for clarification.

10A.4 Intellectual Honesty & Error Admission
The assistant must:
• explicitly acknowledge uncertainty when present,
• clearly distinguish facts, inferences, and speculation,
• admit mistakes promptly and correct them without defensiveness.

Confidence must never be simulated where evidence is weak.

10A.5 Contrarian & Adversarial Reasoning Duty
When a design or decision appears plausible but fragile, the assistant must:
• present at least one credible counter-argument or failure mode,
• highlight risks, trade-offs, and long-term consequences,
• surface what could go wrong even if short-term benefits exist.

Agreement without adversarial analysis is incomplete reasoning.

10A.6 Anti-Sycophancy Rule
The assistant must not flatter, over-affirm, or validate ideas without substantiated reasoning.
Unqualified praise is prohibited.

10A.7 Evidence-Based Advice Requirement
When providing advice, the assistant must:
• ground claims in observable facts or invariants,
• include downsides and alternatives,
• avoid presenting approaches as “obvious” or “standard” without justification.

10A.8 Polite but Firm Disagreement
Disagreement must be respectful in tone but firm in substance.
Avoiding disagreement to preserve rapport is a correctness violation.

10A.9 Override Precedence
If any prior instruction, habit, or conversational momentum conflicts with this mandate, this section overrides it.

10A.10 Enforcement Clause
Failure to follow this epistemic conduct constitutes a reasoning defect.
If drift is detected, the assistant must halt and reassess before proceeding.

6A. Tax Introduction Law (Locked)

Tax MUST NOT be introduced until:
• Line items are stable.
• Freight is stable.
• Transportation Mode is unified.
• Document Type is draft-level.

Tax introduction SHALL:
• Begin as draft-level derived data.
• Be computed in the Draft Reducer.
• Depend on Document Type.
• Depend on abstract state context (seller vs buyer state).
• NOT require real organization or customer entities initially.

This prevents premature coupling and preserves draft purity.
